How this is meant to work

Independent preparation becomes useful when you bring it back into real English.

Use the site to build knowledge, language and strategies at your own pace. Then reuse them actively with your teacher and classmates and put them under pressure in a real colle. Feedback gives you the next thing to work on.

1

Prepare

Use feedback

Come back with a clearer idea of what to consolidate, correct or explore next.

Three places, one progression

The site, your English class and your colles work together.

None of these spaces does exactly the same job. The strongest progress comes from moving between them instead of treating online work as a separate subject.

On the website

Prepare and target

  • Build knowledge and vocabulary.
  • Practise grammar and pronunciation.
  • Identify weaknesses.
  • Prepare documents and oral strategies.
In English class

Interact and experiment

  • Speak before everything feels perfect.
  • Reuse what you prepared.
  • Ask questions and compare ideas.
  • Get immediate guidance and correction.
In your colles

Apply and receive feedback

  • Work under real time pressure.
  • Organise your thinking aloud.
  • Use knowledge and language flexibly.
  • Turn feedback into your next learning goal.
Your English classes and colles are not an optional extra: they are where preparation becomes real language use.

Where should I start?

Choose the route that matches what you need today.

You do not need to explore everything at once. Start with one purpose, follow a short route and then bring something concrete into class or your next colle.

I’m new to the site

Start with the homepage and the guided orientation. See the main tools, choose one short activity and learn how progress is organised.

Best first goal: understand the learning cycle rather than trying every section.

I find English difficult and need a clear plan

Use the Guided Learning Plan and diagnostics to identify a manageable priority in vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, methodology or civilisation.

Best first goal: work on one priority and then try to use it in class.

I have a colle coming up

Use methodology, civilisation, language tools and the Colle Mission Builder to prepare the actual task rather than revising English in the abstract.

Best first goal: arrive in class with a key question, useful language and at least one point you want to test aloud.

I want to improve regularly

Use the daily English routine, your learning path and short review activities to build continuity between lessons and colles.

Best first goal: do a little often, then reuse it. Regular retrieval beats passive rereading.
